`bjt` `electronics`

A Bipolar Junction Transistor is a current-controlled device: a small current into the base controls a much larger current flowing between collector and emitter. That current-in/current-out relationship (rather than a MOSFET's voltage-in) makes BJTs common in simple switching and amplification circuits, though for driving higher-current loads efficiently, MOSFETs are generally preferred due to lower conduction losses.
